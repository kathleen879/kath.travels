10 Amazing Things To Do In Hong Kong

In my time living here in Hong Kong, these are my favourite things to do.

I did little research before coming to Hong Kong for a semester long student exchange programme. In my memory, Hong Kong was merely a land of Dim Sum, Egglets and Mahjong. Of course, I later realised I was devastatingly wrong, because Hong Kong has so much more to offer.

1. Take a stroll along Victoria Harbour

I remember vividly, the Victoria Harbour skyline left me speechless. It was just stunning. When my parents came over to Hong Kong to visit me, it was the first place I brought them to as well. Go and see for yourself. And while you are at it, be sure to compare hand sizes with celebrities along the Avenue of Stars.

2. Take the Star Ferry

You will notice that there are many different ships or cruises you can take, costing about 40HKD to 60HKD. The Star Ferry though, I believe is the best choice especially for broke students like you and me.


It costs a very affordable amount of 5 HKD and you can get on it by simply tapping your Octopus card at the gantry. It is a 5 minute ride to Central on Hong Kong Island and trust me, it would be one of the best 5 minutes during your time in Hong Kong. I strongly encourage you to sit on the second deck for a better view and to feel the cool breeze on your face.

3. Go on the Peak Tram to Victoria Peak

The Peak Tram is one of the oldest railways in the world. It is a 10 minute ride from Central all the way up to Victoria Peak, up a steep slope. If you can, try to get the seat on the right going up for the best views.

4. Heal your inner child at Hong Kong Disneyland

Wow, I loved Disneyland. Hong Kong Disneyland is the first Disneyland I've been to as well! I grew up watching a lot of Disney and Pixar, so I truly was on cloud nine in the most magical place on Earth. My favourite was the Frozen ride.



5. Marvel at the Big Buddha on Lantau Island

The Big Buddha is a large bronze statue, sitting 482 metres above sea level. Visit Ngong Ping Village and Po Lin Monastery while you are at Lantau Island as well.


You can take the Ngong Ping 360 cable car, a bus or even hike up to Ngong Ping Village. I took the cable car with my parents. I remember we queued about an hour for it. If you want to skip the queue, I would suggest getting the Crystal Cabin or the Crystal+ Cabin. It is more expensive than the regular cable car, but personally, I believe it is a good deal as you get a glass bottom for a direct view of the valleys below while also getting a significantly shorter waiting time.


If you are feeling adventurous though, you could attempt the Ngong Ping Rescue Trail which I went on with my friends. Just be careful, it is not an easy hike. The views along the way were stunning.

6. See dried pufferfish at Tai O Fishing Village

If seeing Nemo's dear friend puffed up and sun-dried sounds intriguing to you, then you could explore Tai O Fishing Village. It is a 20 minute bus ride on Bus 21 from Ngong Ping Village Bus Terminal. There are many stalls selling goods such as dried shrimp, seaweed and grilled cuttlefish. My mum bought 5 bags of dried Fish Maw when she visited.


There are other stalls selling fresh seafood which you can purchase and bring to the nearby restuarants to cook. My friends and I arrived in Tai O Fishing Village about 5pm to 6pm, and unfortunately, most of the stalls were done for the day. We didn't manage to get the fresh seafood for dinner and maybe should have came earlier at 1pm or 2pm instead. However, we still had a delicious meal. Take note, it is definitely on the pricier end.

7. Hike along Dragon's Back

This is one of Hong Kong's most iconic hikes, resembling the backbone of a sleeping dragon. It is relatively easy. The first time I went to hike at Dragon's Back, the weather was not in my favour and it was extremely foggy. I had to come back a second time and of course, the views did not disappoint.

8. Climb Lion's Rock

Lion's Rock is a 495m mountain resembling the silhoutte of a lion. If you are into cool rock formations, then I believe climbing Lion's Rock is a must-do item in your visit to Hong Kong. It is not the easiest hike as well. I remember scrambling to the top of the Lion Head and just seeing a whole group of aunties and uncles in their 70s. Hong Kong's elderly hiking enthusiasts are no joke. They even asked me to stand on one of the rocks that were closer to the edge so that they could help me take a photo! How did they get up there?

9. Visit Chi Lin Nunnery and Nan Lian Gardens

For a more cultural experience, visit Chi Lin Nunnery and the adjacent Nan Lian Gardens. It is nice to take a relaxing stroll here. Both are very accessible from Diamond Hill MTR Station.

10. Visit Hong Kong's Islands

I visited three islands in Hong Kong and each one of them had their own unique selling points. I loved it.

Lamma Island had seafood restaurants and tofu pudding. We ended our day trip playing Bridge and watching the sunset at the beach.


Sharp Island is known for volcanic rock formations that resemble Hong Kong's iconic Polo Bun. It also has a natural 200 metre long tombolo, which is a natural sand levee made up of rocks and sediments which only appears at low tide.


Cheung Chau is the most developed island among the three. Seafood, Mango Mochi and Curry Fishballs are just some of the food sold on the island. If possible, do try to catch the Cheung Chau Bun Festival held around late April to early May annually. The festival features Taoist ceromonies, lion dance, parades, cantonese opera and a bun scrambling competition over the period of 5 days.

*Bonus* - Watch a Cantonese Movie

Watching a Cantonese Movie in Hong Kong just hits different. I watched all of the Cold War Collection (Cold War, Cold War 2, Cold War 1994) while I was in Hong Kong. The experience is truly one of a kind. The upcoming Cold War 1995 movie is slated to be released in 2027. Do catch it if you get the chance!

Thank you for reading! I hope you enjoy your time in Hong Kong as much as I did.
